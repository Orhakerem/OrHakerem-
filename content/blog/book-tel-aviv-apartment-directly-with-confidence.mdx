Booking an apartment directly can be a calm, transparent experience when you know what to check. The goal is not to skip the questions that a booking platform would normally surface; it is to ask them clearly, receive a written answer and compare the same stay on the same terms before deciding.

For a Tel Aviv trip, a direct booking can be especially useful when you have specific dates, a group with different needs, a longer stay or questions about the neighbourhood. You speak to the host team, explain what you need and receive a quote based on the actual request. This guide sets out a practical process for doing that with confidence.

## 1. Start with the property, not the price

First confirm that the apartment genuinely fits the trip. Look at the location, bedrooms or sleeping layout, kitchen, outdoor space and the kind of stay it supports. A lower quote is not useful if the property creates compromises you did not expect.

At Or Hakerem, start by comparing the available [properties](/properties). The penthouse is designed for a larger group and has outdoor space; the studio is a more compact central option. Read the complete property page, look through the photographs and note anything that matters to your group before you request dates.

Location also changes the value of a stay. If you want the beach, Carmel Market and central Tel Aviv within easy reach, read our guide to [where to stay in Tel Aviv](/blog/where-to-stay-in-tel-aviv-neighborhoods-guide) before you compare properties across the city.

## 2. Ask for a quote for your exact dates

Vacation rental pricing is date-specific. Weekends, seasonality, length of stay and cleaning can all change the total. The reliable comparison is therefore a quote for the same dates, the same number of people and the same apartment — not a percentage claim or a headline rate.

Use the [reservation page](/reservation) to send your check-in date, check-out date and preferred apartment. The booking team will review the request and provide the relevant details. If your dates are flexible, say so: a small adjustment can sometimes make it easier to find the stay that suits you.

Before moving forward, make sure the quote states the full total, the stay dates, the property and any clearly applicable fees. If something is not clear, ask. Direct booking is strongest when both sides have the same written understanding.

## 3. Confirm availability and the booking process

A direct enquiry is not the same as a confirmed reservation. Until you have received confirmation from the host, treat dates as requested rather than secured. Ask what the next step is, how long the quote is valid and what is needed to complete the reservation.

This is also the moment to share details that affect the stay: estimated arrival time, the number of guests, children, luggage, accessibility needs or a request connected to Shabbat. For a month or more in the city, see our [long-term and monthly stays guide](/blog/long-term-monthly-stays-tel-aviv) and include your preferred length of stay in the enquiry.

Clear communication is a trust signal, not an inconvenience. A responsive host should be able to explain what is included, what needs confirmation and what information they need from you.

## 4. Check independent information as well as the property page

Look for information from more than one source. The official property page is the best place for the apartment details, while public reviews and map listings can help you understand how the business presents itself elsewhere.

You can view the official [Or Hakerem Google Business Profile](https://www.google.com/maps?cid=11119085925362597877) for the business location and guest feedback. Read reviews thoughtfully: a small number of detailed, relevant comments can be more useful than a large total with little context. If you are comparing a platform booking with a direct enquiry, read the [booking-platform comparison guide](/blog/book-direct-vs-airbnb-or-hakerem) for the practical differences without replacing the need to compare your exact quote.

## 5. Keep the important details in writing

Once you decide to proceed, keep the essential information together: the confirmed address, dates, guest count, contact person, total, arrival process and any agreed special arrangements. Save the confirmation and make sure every traveller who needs it has the arrival information.

This is particularly helpful if one person makes the booking while another arrives first. It also avoids the most common travel problem: someone assuming a detail was discussed but not actually confirmed.

## Use a short pre-booking checklist

Before you make a final decision, pause for a five-minute checklist. Confirm that the map location is the one you expect, that the photographs and sleeping arrangement match your group, and that the dates in the quote are correct. Check whether you have asked about anything that could affect arrival, such as a late flight, a child travelling with you or a need for extra clarity around the first evening. This is not about turning a holiday into paperwork. It is about making sure the practical details are settled before the journey starts, leaving you free to enjoy Tel Aviv once you arrive.

It is also worth checking that your contact details are correct and that you have given the host a way to reach you if an arrival question comes up. If your itinerary changes after you send the request, update the host rather than assuming the original timing still works. A small message before travel is much easier than resolving a misunderstanding at the door.

Finally, give yourself time to read the confirmation rather than approving it while travelling between flights or meetings. A calm review of the details is one of the simplest ways to begin a stay with confidence.

For a more complex trip — multiple apartments, a longer stay, a family visit or guests arriving at different times — include the full context in the reservation request. A short, complete message is faster to answer than several fragmented questions.

## 6. Make the first day easy

Direct booking gives you the opportunity to plan the practical side of arrival before you travel. Decide how you will reach the property, what you need on the first evening and whether you want to shop for food once you arrive. Keep the arrival instructions accessible to everyone in your travel party, especially if flights land at different times.

If your arrival is close to Shabbat or a holiday, ask your questions early. If you are visiting for a celebration or have guests joining you, share the timing and group details from the start. The more accurately the host understands the trip, the more useful their advice can be.

## A direct booking should feel clear from start to finish

The best direct booking experience is simple: choose the right property, send the real dates, receive a clear quote, ask questions, confirm the reservation and keep the details in writing. There is no need for pressure or vague promises. A useful final check is to ask yourself whether you could explain the plan to another traveller in one minute: where you are staying, when you arrive, what the total covers and who to contact. If any of those answers are unclear, ask before confirming.
